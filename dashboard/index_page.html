The evidence behind the tiers

Whether a stock reaches +10% or +20% within six weeks depends mostly on how much it already moves each day. Across — weekly tests since 2017, Double tier stocks reached +20% before their stop — of the time and Single tier stocks reached +10% — of the time. Stage, sector stage and market regime did not predict results consistently, so they are shown here for information and are not used to filter. Stops and targets did not beat simply holding in testing, so treat them as loss limits, not as a source of profit.

The odds rise with daily range

Share of trades that reached the target before the stop, grouped by the stock's average daily range (average true range as a share of price) on the day of entry. Each trade ran up to 30 trading days. Stops sit below the 10 day low or two daily ranges under price, never closer than 3%.

Wider daily range means wider stops, so more volatile stocks win more often but risk more per trade. The Double tier starts at a 4.0% daily range and the Single tier at 2.8%.

Do stops and targets help?

The same trades replayed under other exit rules. Holding 30 days with no stop earned more per day than any rule with a stop, but its worst trades lost more than twice as much, so the stop mainly limits damage. Rules that let winners run depend on a few very large winners, and a list of stocks that are over $2B today overstates those. For that reason none of these rules is built into the screener.

How it works

The goal

Find liquid stocks that can gain 10% (a single) or 20% (a double) within two to six weeks, so that a handful of 20% moves a year can compound a portfolio.

Two tiers

Stocks over $2B with a price over $10 and $20M traded a day are split by average daily range: 4.0% or more is the Double tier, 2.8% to under 4.0% is the Single tier. Range was the one measure that predicted target hits in every year tested.

How trades were tested

Every week from September 2017 to August 2026, each qualifying stock was entered at the close. The trade ended at the target, at the stop, or after 30 trading days. If both traded on one day the stop was assumed first, and gaps through the stop filled at the open.

What did not help

Stage, sector stage, the market regime from SPY, an uptrend filter, tight base and pullback patterns, volume, and a stock's past record of big moves all failed to help in both halves of the data. A past record of moves raised win rate but cut profit per trade.

Limits

Only stocks that are over $2B today and have survived are included, which flatters the absolute results, so rely on comparisons rather than the level of profit. Costs and taxes are not counted, earnings dates are not checked, and stocks with under about a year of history are skipped. This is research, not investment advice.

Data

Daily prices come from Nasdaq's public data, ten years for every stock in the universe, refreshed by rerunning the fetch, engine and screener scripts in that order.
